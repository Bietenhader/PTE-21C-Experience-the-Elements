Hydrogen is the lightest element, a colourless gas of two-atom molecules and the main fuel of the stars; on Earth we meet it mostly bound into water and the molecules of living things. Its ordinary nucleus is a single proton, but about 0.0145 % of natural hydrogen is deuterium, whose nucleus also holds a neutron and which makes 'heavy water'. With one electron in the 1s orbital (ground term ²S₁/₂), hydrogen is the only neutral atom whose Schrödinger equation can be solved exactly. The deep red line in a hydrogen lamp's glow is the Balmer line at 656.3 nm, emitted as the electron falls from the third shell to the second.
