Neon is a noble gas present in air only in traces and obtained by distilling liquefied air. Its fame comes from glowing signs: an electric current through neon at low pressure gives a warm red-orange light, and only that colour is true neon, the other shades coming from different gases or from coatings on the tube. Neon's ten electrons fill the first two shells completely ([He] 2s² 2p⁶, ground term ¹S₀), and only helium holds its outermost electron more tightly, with neon's first ionisation energy at 2081 kJ/mol. No stable chemical compound of neon is known.
