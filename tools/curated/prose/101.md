Mendelevium is a synthetic actinide named after Dmitri Mendeleev, the chemist who arranged the elements into the periodic table. It was made at Berkeley in 1955 by striking a minute target of einsteinium-253 with helium ions, and it was the first element identified one atom at a time; the isotope produced, mendelevium-256, has a half-life of about 78 minutes. Even its longest-lived isotope, mendelevium-258, lasts only about 52 days, so its chemistry is known only from trace experiments, which reveal a +2 state alongside the usual +3. The atom, [Rn] 5f¹³ 7s², is a single electron short of a full 5f shell, giving a ²F°₇/₂ ground term.
