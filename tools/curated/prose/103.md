Lawrencium is a synthetic element that ends the actinide series and, in many modern tables, sits in group 3 beneath lutetium. A Berkeley team first reported it in 1961 after bombarding about three micrograms of californium with boron ions; it honours Ernest Lawrence, inventor of the cyclotron, and its first proposed symbol, Lw, was changed to Lr. Its outermost electron does something unexpected. Calculations, supported by experiment, place it in a 7p orbital, [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 7s² 7p¹, rather than in the 6d orbital that the Aufbau rule predicts, a relativistic effect. Accordingly, its first ionisation energy, measured on only a few atoms at a time at about 479 kJ/mol, is lower than sodium's.
