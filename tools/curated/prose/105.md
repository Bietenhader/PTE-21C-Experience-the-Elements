Dubnium is a synthetic element in group 5, below tantalum, named after the Russian town of Dubna, whose laboratory reported it in 1967; a Berkeley team claimed it independently in 1970. The rivalry fed years of argument over names, and the Berkeley proposal, hahnium, was eventually abandoned. Only a few atoms are made in any experiment, yet dubnium-268 is remarkably durable for so heavy a nucleus: its half-life of about 28 hours is the longest of any known isotope beyond lawrencium. Theory predicts the outer configuration [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d³ 7s² and a ⁴F₃/₂ ground level, the same pattern as tantalum, but neither has been measured.
