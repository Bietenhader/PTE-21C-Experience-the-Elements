Seaborgium is a synthetic element in group 6, below tungsten, first made at Berkeley in 1974 by firing oxygen-18 ions at californium-249; its atoms of seaborgium-263 lasted about a second. It honours Glenn Seaborg, co-discoverer of plutonium and several other heavy elements, and the name was given while he was still alive, a distinction it shares only with oganesson. Only a few atoms have ever been made, and its longest-lived isotopes survive for roughly a minute or two. Theory assigns it tungsten's outer arrangement, [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁴ 7s², with a ground level of total angular momentum J = 0, but nothing about its electrons has been measured directly.
