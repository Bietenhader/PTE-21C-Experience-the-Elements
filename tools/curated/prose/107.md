Bohrium is a synthetic element in group 7, below rhenium, named after the Danish physicist Niels Bohr. A Dubna team reported it in 1976, and in 1981 a group at the GSI laboratory in Darmstadt made it convincingly by fusing bismuth-209 with chromium-54, identifying six atoms of bohrium-262, whose half-life is about a tenth of a second. Chemists have since turned single atoms of a longer-lived isotope into a volatile oxychloride that behaves like rhenium's. Only small numbers of atoms exist, its heavier isotopes lasting from seconds to about a minute. Theory predicts [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁵ 7s² with a ground level of J = 5/2, matching rhenium's half-filled d shell, though none of this has been measured.
