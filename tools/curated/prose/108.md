Hassium is a synthetic element in group 8, below osmium, named after Hesse, the German state in which Darmstadt lies. It was first made there at GSI in 1984 by fusing lead-208 with iron-58, giving atoms of hassium-265 with a half-life of about 2 milliseconds. Only a few atoms have been made since, yet chemists have turned a handful of them into a volatile tetroxide, just as osmium forms OsO₄. Its nucleus is notable too: hassium-270, with 108 protons and 162 neutrons, is regarded as a doubly magic deformed nucleus, and it survives for several seconds. Theory predicts the atom's configuration as [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁶ 7s², but it has not been measured.
