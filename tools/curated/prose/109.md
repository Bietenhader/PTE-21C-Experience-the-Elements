Meitnerium is a synthetic element in group 9, below iridium, named after Lise Meitner, who helped to explain nuclear fission; it is the only element named after a woman alone. It was first made at GSI in Darmstadt on 29 August 1982, when a week of bombarding bismuth-209 with iron-58 produced a single fused nucleus of meitnerium-266, an isotope with a half-life of about 2 milliseconds. Only a few atoms have been made since; its heavier isotopes survive for seconds, and its chemistry has never been studied. The configuration [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁷ 7s², the same outer pattern as iridium, is a theoretical prediction, not a measurement.
