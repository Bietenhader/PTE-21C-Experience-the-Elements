Sodium is a soft, silvery alkali metal that can be cut with a knife and must be kept under oil, because it reacts vigorously with water; we meet it every day in table salt and as the ions that help carry signals along our nerves. It melts at about 371 K (98 °C), below the boiling point of water. The yellow glow of salt in a flame, and of old street lamps, is a pair of lines at 589.0 and 589.6 nm. Both come from the lone 3s electron ([Ne] 3s¹) falling back from 3p, a level split in two by spin–orbit coupling into ²P°₃/₂ and ²P°₁/₂.
