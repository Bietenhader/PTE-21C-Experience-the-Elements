Roentgenium is a synthetic element in group 11, the heavy sibling of copper, silver and gold, named after Wilhelm Röntgen, who discovered X-rays. A team at GSI in Darmstadt made it in late 1994 by fusing bismuth-209 with nickel-64 and identified three atoms of roentgenium-272, an isotope with a half-life of about 4 milliseconds. Only small numbers of atoms have been produced since; roentgenium-282 lasts around 100 seconds, and its chemistry is unexplored. Gold has the famous 5d¹⁰ 6s¹ arrangement, but for roentgenium calculations predict [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁹ 7s², because relativity stabilises the 7s orbital so strongly that it holds two electrons while 6d keeps a vacancy.
