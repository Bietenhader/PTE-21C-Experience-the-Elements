Copernicium is a synthetic element in group 12, below zinc, cadmium and mercury, named after the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus. It was first made at GSI in Darmstadt on 9 February 1996 by fusing lead-208 with zinc-70; its first atoms, of copernicium-277, lasted less than a millisecond. Heavier isotopes live longer, copernicium-285 for about half a minute, and experiments on single atoms show it to be an extremely volatile metal, even more so than mercury. Only small numbers of atoms have ever been made. Calculations predict a closed-shell [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² configuration, with relativity binding the 7s pair so tightly that some studies expect copernicium to be a liquid at room temperature.
