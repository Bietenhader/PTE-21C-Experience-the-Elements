Nihonium is a synthetic element in group 13, below thallium, and the first element discovered in an Asian country; its name comes from Nihon, one way of saying 'Japan' in Japanese. At the RIKEN laboratory in Wako, researchers fired zinc-70 ions at bismuth-209 and caught their first atom on 23 July 2004; collecting only a handful of convincing atoms of nihonium-278, half-life about 1.4 milliseconds, took years. A heavier isotope, nihonium-286, lasts about 10 seconds. Its predicted configuration is [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p¹, with the lone outer electron in the strongly stabilised 7p₁/₂ level; no measurement exists, and its chemistry is barely explored.
