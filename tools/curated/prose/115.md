Moscovium is a synthetic element in group 15, below bismuth, named after the Moscow region, home of the Dubna laboratory where it was made. Between July and August 2003 a Russian–American team fired calcium-48 ions at americium-243 and recorded four atoms, which decayed within a fraction of a second; the results were announced in February 2004. Its longest-lived known isotope, moscovium-290, has a half-life of only about 0.65 seconds, and only small numbers of atoms have ever been made. The predicted configuration, [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p³, places a single electron above a closed 7p₁/₂ pair, so theorists expect a +1 state to matter far more than it does for bismuth.
