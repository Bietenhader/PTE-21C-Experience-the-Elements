Oganesson is the heaviest element known, a synthetic member of group 18 below radon, named after the nuclear physicist Yuri Oganessian, who was alive when it was named; only seaborgium shares that distinction. Its discovery was announced in October 2006 by the Dubna laboratory and Lawrence Livermore: a long bombardment of californium-249 with calcium-48 ions had yielded just three atoms of oganesson-294, an isotope with a half-life of about half a millisecond. No chemistry has been possible. Its predicted configuration fills the 7p shell, [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p⁶, yet calculations suggest relativity blurs its shell structure and that it might even be a solid, not a gas, at room temperature.
