Magnesium is a light, silvery alkaline-earth metal that burns with a dazzling white flame, once used in photographers' flashes; alloyed, it lightens car and aircraft parts. Its most important place in biology is the centre of every chlorophyll molecule, where a single magnesium ion is gripped by four nitrogen atoms of the surrounding ring; leaves short of magnesium turn yellow between their veins. The atom has the closed configuration [Ne] 3s² (ground term ¹S₀). Of its three stable isotopes, only magnesium-25, about 10 % of the total, has a nuclear spin (5/2); the other two have none.
