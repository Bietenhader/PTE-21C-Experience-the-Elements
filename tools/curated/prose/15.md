Phosphorus is a reactive nonmetal found in several forms: waxy white phosphorus, which must be kept under water, the red phosphorus of matchbox strikers, and violet and black varieties. Life cannot do without it, in DNA, in the energy carrier ATP, in bones, and as one of the three main nutrients in crop fertilisers. In 1669 the alchemist Hennig Brand isolated it by boiling down large amounts of urine; the green glow of white phosphorus in air comes from slow oxidation, a kind of chemiluminescence, not from phosphorescence. The atom has a half-filled 3p subshell, [Ne] 3s² 3p³ (ground term ⁴S°₃/₂), and phosphorus-31, its only stable isotope, has nuclear spin 1/2, ideal for NMR.
