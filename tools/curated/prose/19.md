Potassium is a soft alkali metal that reacts so violently with water that the hydrogen released catches fire, its flame tinted lilac by the potassium. Living things depend on it: plants use potassium to open and close the pores in their leaves, which is why potash is a major fertiliser, and our nerves rely on it too. A small fraction, 0.0117 %, of natural potassium is radioactive potassium-40, with a half-life of about 1.25 billion years, so every human body is faintly radioactive. The nineteenth electron enters 4s rather than 3d, giving [Ar] 4s¹ (ground term ²S₁/₂): the first place in the table where a new shell starts before the one beneath is full.
