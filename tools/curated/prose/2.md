Helium is a noble gas, the second lightest element and the second most common in the universe; on Earth it is collected from natural gas, where it has gathered after being released as alpha particles by uranium and thorium decaying in rocks. It was first spotted in 1868 as a yellow line near 587.6 nm in the light of the Sun, long before anyone found it on Earth. Its two electrons fill the 1s shell (ground term ¹S₀), giving helium the highest first ionisation energy of any element, about 2372 kJ/mol, so it forms no stable compounds under ordinary conditions.
