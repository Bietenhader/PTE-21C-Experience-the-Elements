Calcium is a reactive alkaline-earth metal that we almost never see pure; instead we meet it in bones and teeth, in seashells, chalk, limestone and marble, and in the pectin 'glue' that holds neighbouring plant cells together. Calcium-48, only 0.187 % of natural calcium, is remarkably rich in neutrons yet almost stable, decaying with a half-life of about 2.9 × 10¹⁹ years. The reason is a nuclear shell effect: its 20 protons and 28 neutrons are both 'magic numbers' that close nuclear shells, so it is doubly magic. Beams of calcium-48 were used to create the heaviest elements yet made. The atom itself is [Ar] 4s², ground term ¹S₀.
