Scandium is a light, silvery transition metal, named after Scandinavia, where its minerals were found; small additions of it strengthen the aluminium alloys used in some aircraft parts and sports equipment. Its existence was foreseen: Mendeleev left a gap in his table for an element he called eka-boron and predicted its properties, and scandium, discovered in 1879, filled that gap closely. Scandium is the first element to place an electron in a 3d orbital, giving [Ar] 3d¹ 4s² and the ground term ²D₃/₂, yet in compounds it almost always gives away all three outer electrons; with no d electrons left, its compounds are usually colourless.
