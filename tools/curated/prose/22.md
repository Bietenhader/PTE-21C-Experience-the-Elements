Titanium is a transition metal as strong as many steels but much lighter, and it shrugs off seawater and body fluids; we meet it in aircraft, spectacle frames and hip implants, onto which living bone grows firmly. Surprisingly, most mined titanium never becomes metal at all: it is turned into titanium dioxide, the brilliant white pigment in paint, paper and sunscreen. Radioactive titanium-44, with a half-life of about 59 years, is forged in exploding stars, and its gamma rays have been detected from young supernova remnants. The atom, [Ar] 3d² 4s², has two unpaired 3d electrons and the ground term ³F₂.
