Vanadium is a hard, silvery transition metal whose main job is to make steel tougher, in tools, springs and axles; it is also the heart of large 'flow' batteries that store electricity from wind and sun. It was named after Vanadís, a Norse goddess of beauty, because of its colourful compounds: in solution it steps from yellow through blue and green to violet as its oxidation state falls from +5 to +2. The atom, [Ar] 3d³ 4s² (ground term ⁴F₃/₂), has three unpaired d electrons, and the rare isotope vanadium-50 is barely radioactive, with a half-life of about 2.65 × 10¹⁷ years and an unusually high nuclear spin of 6.
