Chromium is a hard, shiny transition metal; a thin layer of it gives taps and bumpers their mirror finish, and mixed into steel it forms a self-healing oxide skin that makes the steel 'stainless'. Its name comes from the Greek for colour, and fittingly the red of ruby and much of the green of emerald come from small traces of chromium in the crystal. Chromium also breaks the simple filling rule: the Aufbau order predicts [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s², but measurement shows [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹, a half-filled d subshell plus one s electron. That leaves six unpaired electrons, the most of any element in its row, and the ground term ⁷S₃.
