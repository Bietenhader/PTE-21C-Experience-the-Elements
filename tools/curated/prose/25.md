Manganese is a hard, brittle, grey transition metal; most of it goes into steel, which it toughens, and it also forms the dark manganese dioxide inside ordinary alkaline batteries. Life uses it in a remarkable place: in photosystem II of plants and algae, a cluster of four manganese atoms and one calcium atom pulls electrons from water, and almost all the oxygen in the air was released there. The atom has a half-filled 3d subshell, [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s², with five unpaired electrons whose spins all point the same way; their orbital motions cancel, giving an S-type ground term, ⁶S₅/₂.
