Cobalt is a hard, bluish-silver metal and one of the few elements that are magnetic at room temperature; it gives glass and porcelain their deep 'cobalt blue' and sits in the cathodes of many lithium-ion batteries. Its name comes from the Kobold, a goblin that German miners blamed for ores that gave off poisonous arsenic fumes instead of useful metal. Cobalt is the metal at the heart of vitamin B₁₂, and the bacteria in legume root nodules need it to fix nitrogen. The atom, [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² (ground term ⁴F₉/₂), has three unpaired d electrons, and its single stable isotope, cobalt-59, has nuclear spin 7/2.
