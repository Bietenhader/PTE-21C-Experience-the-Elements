Copper is a soft, reddish metal that conducts electricity better than any other except silver, which is why it runs through our walls as wiring; alloyed with tin it gave the Bronze Age its name, and with zinc it becomes brass. Plants need traces of it too, for plastocyanin, a small copper protein that ferries electrons in photosynthesis. Copper breaks the simple filling rule: the Aufbau order predicts [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s², but measurement shows [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹, a completely filled d subshell plus one s electron (ground term ²S₁/₂). In the metal, those d electrons absorb blue and green light, leaving copper its warm colour.
