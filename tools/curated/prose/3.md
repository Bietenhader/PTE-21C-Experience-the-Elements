Lithium is the lightest metal, a soft, silvery alkali metal that tarnishes quickly in moist air; today we meet it mainly inside the rechargeable batteries of phones and electric cars. With a density of only 0.534 g/cm³ it is the least dense solid element and would float on water, although it reacts with the water as it does so. Its configuration [He] 2s¹ leaves one outer electron alone in the 2s orbital (ground term ²S₁/₂). When that electron is lifted to 2p and falls back, it emits the deep red line near 670.8 nm that colours lithium flames crimson.
