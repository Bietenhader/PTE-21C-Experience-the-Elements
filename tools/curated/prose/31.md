Gallium is a soft, silvery metal that melts at 302.91 K (about 30 °C), so a lump turns liquid in a warm hand, yet it does not boil until 2477 K; like water, it expands as it freezes. It was discovered in 1875 through two new violet lines, at 403.3 and 417.2 nm, in the spectrum of a zinc ore, and today gallium nitride makes the blue and white LEDs in lamps and screens. The atom is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹ (ground term ²P°₁/₂). Its filled 3d subshell shields the nucleus poorly, so its first ionisation energy, 579 kJ/mol, almost equals aluminium's despite eighteen extra protons.
