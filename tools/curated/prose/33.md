Arsenic is a grey, brittle metalloid, infamous as a poison yet useful in the gallium arsenide chips of mobile phones and laser diodes. Rice can carry more of it than other cereals, because in flooded paddy soils dissolved arsenite slips into roots through the channels the plant uses to take up silicon. Heated at normal pressure, solid arsenic never melts: it turns straight into vapour at about 887 K and becomes liquid only under pressure. Like nitrogen and phosphorus above it, arsenic has three unpaired p electrons, one in each 4p orbital ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³, ground term ⁴S°₃/₂), and nature makes only one stable isotope, arsenic-75.
