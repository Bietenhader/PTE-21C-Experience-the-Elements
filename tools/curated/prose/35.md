Bromine is a halogen and one of only two elements that are liquid at room temperature, the other being mercury: a dark red-brown liquid giving off an orange, foul-smelling vapour, which earned it a name from the Greek for 'stench'. Surprisingly, the Tyrian purple prized by ancient emperors, extracted from sea snails, is a bromine compound. The atom, [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁵ (ground term ²P°₃/₂), lacks one electron to fill its shell. Natural bromine is an almost even mix of two stable isotopes, 50.65 % bromine-79 and 49.35 % bromine-81, so molecules containing it show a tell-tale pair of nearly equal peaks in a mass spectrum.
