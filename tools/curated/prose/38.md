Strontium is a soft, silvery alkaline-earth metal named after Strontian, a village in the Scottish Highlands where its ore was found; its salts give fireworks and distress flares their crimson red. Because the body mistakes it for calcium, radioactive strontium-90 from nuclear fallout, with a half-life of about 29 years, lodges in bones. The atom has two paired outer electrons, [Kr] 5s² (ground term ¹S₀), and its strongest visible line, at 460.7 nm, is blue. A far fainter transition, from the ground state to a long-lived triplet level, is so sharp that strontium optical clocks are among the most precise timekeepers ever built.
