Beryllium is a light, stiff, steel-grey metal of the alkaline-earth group, found in the mineral beryl, of which emerald and aquamarine are coloured varieties. Because X-rays pass through it easily, thin beryllium sheets serve as windows in X-ray tubes, and gold-coated beryllium forms the mirror segments of the James Webb Space Telescope. Beryllium has only one stable isotope, beryllium-9, whose nucleus has spin 3/2; the lighter beryllium-8 falls apart into two helium nuclei almost instantly, a bottleneck stars must overcome to make carbon. Its four electrons fill the 1s and 2s orbitals, giving [He] 2s² and the ground term ¹S₀.
