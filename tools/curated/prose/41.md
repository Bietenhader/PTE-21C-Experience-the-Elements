Niobium is a soft, grey, ductile transition metal, named after Niobe, daughter of Tantalus in Greek myth, because it is so hard to tell apart from its chemical twin, tantalum. Its most surprising job is in hospitals: the coils of most MRI scanners are wound from niobium–titanium wire, which carries current with zero resistance when chilled by liquid helium, and pure niobium itself becomes a superconductor below about 9 K. Its electrons break the simple filling rule: the measured ground configuration is [Kr] 4d⁴ 5s¹, not the predicted 4d³ 5s², leaving five unpaired electrons and the ground term ⁶D₁/₂.
