Molybdenum is a hard, silvery transition metal that melts at 2896 K and is mostly used to make steels stronger and tougher at high temperature. Surprisingly, life depends on it: the usual form of nitrogenase, the enzyme with which root-nodule bacteria turn nitrogen from the air into ammonia, carries molybdenum at its heart, and plants need it for nitrate reductase, so molybdenum-poor soils can leave crops short of nitrogen. Like chromium above it, molybdenum breaks the filling rule: its ground state is [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹ rather than 4d⁴ 5s², with six unpaired electrons and the ground term ⁷S₃.
