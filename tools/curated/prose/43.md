Technetium is a silvery transition metal with no stable isotopes; in 1937 it became the first element made artificially, from molybdenum bombarded in a cyclotron, and its name comes from the Greek for ‘artificial’. A short-lived excited form of technetium-99 is now the most widely used tracer in nuclear-medicine scans. Even its longest-lived isotopes survive only about four million years, so when astronomers found technetium lines in the light of certain red giant stars, it proved that stars forge new elements. Its ground configuration, [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s², follows the filling rule and leaves a half-filled 4d shell (⁶S₅/₂).
