Ruthenium is a hard, silvery platinum-group metal, recovered as a by-product of platinum and nickel refining and named after Ruthenia, a Latin name for Russia, because it was first isolated from platinum ore from the Ural Mountains. A pinch goes a long way: about a tenth of a per cent of ruthenium makes titanium far more resistant to corrosion, and small additions harden platinum and palladium for hard-wearing electrical contacts. Its atom breaks the filling rule: the measured ground configuration is [Kr] 4d⁷ 5s¹, not the predicted 4d⁶ 5s², giving four unpaired electrons and the ground term ⁵F₅.
