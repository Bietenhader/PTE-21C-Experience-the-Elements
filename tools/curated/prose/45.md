Rhodium is a rare, hard, highly reflective platinum-group metal. Most of it goes into the catalytic converters of cars, where it helps turn nitrogen oxides into harmless nitrogen, and it is electroplated onto white-gold jewellery for a bright finish that does not tarnish. William Hyde Wollaston found it in 1803 and named it after the Greek for rose, for the rose-red colour of its salts. Its electrons defy the simple filling rule: the ground state is [Kr] 4d⁸ 5s¹ rather than 4d⁷ 5s². Its only stable isotope, rhodium-103, has nuclear spin 1/2 but a tiny magnetic moment, making it one of the hardest nuclei to detect by NMR.
