Palladium is a silvery-white platinum-group metal, the least dense and lowest-melting of its family, used in catalytic converters, dental alloys and, mixed with gold, white gold. At room temperature it acts as a metal sponge for hydrogen, soaking up as much as 900 times its own volume of the gas, and heated palladium lets hydrogen slip through while blocking other gases, a neat way to purify it. Its atom is unique: the ground configuration is [Kr] 4d¹⁰, with no electron at all in the fifth shell, even though palladium sits in period 5. The filling rule predicts 4d⁸ 5s², but the completely filled d shell (¹S₀) wins.
