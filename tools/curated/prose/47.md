Silver is a soft, brilliant white metal, prized since antiquity for coins and tableware, and it conducts electricity and heat better than any other metal. It tarnishes not because of oxygen but because traces of sulfur compounds in the air coat it with black silver sulfide. Silver also starred in a founding experiment of quantum physics: about a century ago Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach sent a beam of silver atoms through an uneven magnetic field and saw it split cleanly in two. The cause is a single unpaired 5s electron outside a full 4d shell ([Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹, ground term ²S₁/₂), whose magnetism can point only two ways; the filling rule had predicted 4d⁹ 5s².
