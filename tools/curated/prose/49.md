Indium is a very soft, silvery post-transition metal that a fingernail can dent; most of it now goes into indium tin oxide, the transparent conducting film on touchscreens and flat-panel displays. Oddly, most natural indium is radioactive: indium-115, about 96 per cent of the total, decays so slowly (half-life 4.41 × 10¹⁴ years) that its radioactivity is negligible, while the rare indium-113 is stable. Ferdinand Reich and Theodor Richter discovered the element in 1863 through a brilliant indigo spectral line at 451.1 nm, which gave it its name; the line is emitted when the outer electron drops from the 6s level to the upper fine-structure level of the ground term ²P°.
