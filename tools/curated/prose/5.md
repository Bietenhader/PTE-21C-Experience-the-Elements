Boron is a hard metalloid that appears as black, glittering crystals or a brown powder; we meet it in borax, in heat-resistant borosilicate glassware and, invisibly, in crops and other flowering plants, which need traces of it to hold their cell walls together. About 19.65 % of natural boron is boron-10, a nucleus that captures slow neutrons so readily that it is used in reactor control rods and radiation shielding. The atom's ground configuration, [He] 2s² 2p¹, leaves a single unpaired p electron (ground term ²P°₁/₂), and boron-10 has an unusually large nuclear spin, 3, for so light a nucleus.
