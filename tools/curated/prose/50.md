Tin is a soft, silvery post-transition metal that, alloyed with copper, made the bronze of the Bronze Age; today it goes into solder, the thin coating of steel ‘tin’ cans, and the pools of molten tin on which flat window glass is floated. Below about 13 °C, ordinary white tin can slowly crumble into a grey powder, a change once called tin pest. Its nucleus is special: tin has 50 protons, one of the magic numbers at which nuclear shells close, and this extra stability gives it ten stable isotopes, more than any other element.
