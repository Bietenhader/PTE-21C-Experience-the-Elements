Antimony is a brittle, silvery metalloid that conducts heat and electricity poorly; much of it goes into flame retardants and into the lead alloys of car batteries, which it hardens. People have used it for millennia: in ancient Egypt the black antimony sulfide mineral stibnite was ground into eye make-up, and its Latin name, stibium, gave the element its symbol, Sb. Its outer electrons follow the filling rule, [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p³: the three 5p electrons occupy three separate orbitals with parallel spins, a half-filled shell with ground term ⁴S°₃/₂. Natural antimony is a mixture of two stable isotopes, antimony-121 and antimony-123.
