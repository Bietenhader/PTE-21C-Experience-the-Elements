Tellurium is a rare, brittle, silvery-white metalloid, recovered mainly from the sludge of copper refining and used in cadmium telluride solar panels and in thermoelectric coolers. It defies the ordering of the periodic table by mass: its atomic weight, 127.60, exceeds that of iodine, 126.90, which comes after it, yet Mendeleev rightly put tellurium first because of its chemistry. The reason lies in its nuclei, as most natural tellurium consists of the heavy isotopes 128 and 130. Tellurium-128 is in fact radioactive, decaying by double beta decay with a half-life of about 7.7 × 10²⁴ years, one of the slowest decays ever measured.
