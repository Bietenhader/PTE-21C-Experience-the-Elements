Iodine is a halogen that forms shiny blue-black crystals which give off a violet vapour when warmed; its name comes from the Greek for violet. Humans need it to make thyroid hormones, which is why table salt is often iodised. Bernard Courtois discovered it in 1811 in seaweed ash, and brown seaweeds such as kelp still concentrate iodine from seawater many thousandfold. The atom has seven outer electrons, [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p⁵, one short of a closed shell, so it behaves like a single vacancy in the 5p shell, with ground term ²P°₃/₂. Only one isotope, iodine-127, is stable.
