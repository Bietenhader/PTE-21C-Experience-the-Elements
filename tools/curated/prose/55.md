Caesium is a soft, pale-gold alkali metal that melts at 301.59 K (about 28 °C), so a sealed glass ampoule of it turns liquid in a warm hand; it reacts explosively with water. Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff discovered it in 1860 by analysing mineral water with a spectroscope and named it after the Latin for sky blue, for two blue lines in its spectrum. Its lone 6s electron is the most loosely bound of any element (first ionisation energy 375.7 kJ/mol). It couples to the spin-7/2 nucleus of caesium-133, splitting the ground level in two, and the transition between them, exactly 9 192 631 770 Hz, defines the second.
