Barium is a soft, silvery alkaline-earth metal so reactive that it is kept under oil; its name comes from the Greek for heavy, after its dense ore, barite. Soluble barium salts are poisonous, yet patients swallow a ‘barium meal’ of barium sulfate before X-ray examinations of the gut: the sulfate is too insoluble to be absorbed, while the heavy barium atoms stop X-rays and outline the stomach and intestines. Barium nitrate gives fireworks their green. In the nucleus, barium-138, about 72 per cent of natural barium, has 82 neutrons, a magic number that completes a nuclear shell and makes it especially stable.
