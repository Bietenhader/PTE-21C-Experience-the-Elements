Lanthanum is a soft, silvery metal that gives the lanthanide series its name; it lay hidden in cerium minerals until Carl Gustaf Mosander extracted it in 1839, hence a name from the Greek for ‘to escape notice’. Its oxide goes into high-quality camera-lens glass, and lanthanum–nickel alloys soak up hydrogen reversibly, which is how nickel–metal hydride batteries store their charge. Strangely, the element at the start of the f-block has no f electron at all: its ground configuration is [Xe] 5d¹ 6s², while the filling rule predicts 4f¹ 6s² (ground term ²D₃/₂). A rare natural isotope, lanthanum-138, is radioactive, with a half-life of about 10¹¹ years.
