Cerium is the most abundant of the rare-earth metals, more plentiful in Earth’s crust than copper, and an iron-grey metal so reactive that scratching the pure metal can make it ignite. That is why lighter flints, made of a cerium-rich alloy called mischmetal mixed with iron, throw sparks when struck; cerium oxide is also used to polish glass. Cerium’s 4f and 5d electrons have almost the same energy, so its ground state is [Xe] 4f¹ 5d¹ 6s² instead of the predicted 4f² 6s² (ground term ¹G°₄), and it can give up a fourth electron with ease, making the Ce⁴⁺ ion a strong oxidising agent.
