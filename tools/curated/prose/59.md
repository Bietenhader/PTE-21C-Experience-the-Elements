Praseodymium is a soft, silvery lanthanide that grows a green oxide skin in air; its name means ‘green twin’, because in 1885 Carl Auer von Welsbach split it from ‘didymium’, a supposed element that was really a mixture of praseodymium and neodymium. Its salts give glass and ceramic glazes a clean yellow, and glasses containing both twins are worn by glassblowers to cut out the yellow glare of hot glass. The atom keeps its filling-rule configuration, [Xe] 4f³ 6s², with three unpaired 4f electrons (ground term ⁴I°₉/₂). Its only stable isotope, praseodymium-141, has 82 neutrons, a magic number.
