Carbon is the nonmetal on which all known life is built, and we meet it as soot, pencil graphite and diamond, and in every food, fuel and living cell. Its radioactive isotope carbon-14, made continually by cosmic rays in the upper air, decays with a half-life of 5700 years, which lets archaeologists date wood, bone and seeds up to tens of thousands of years old. A free carbon atom, [He] 2s² 2p², has two unpaired electrons (ground term ³P₀), yet in methane or diamond it forms four equivalent bonds, which chemists describe as one 2s electron being promoted and the orbitals mixing into sp³ hybrids.
