Neodymium is a silvery lanthanide that tarnishes quickly in air and is the key ingredient of neodymium–iron–boron magnets, the strongest permanent magnets in everyday use, found in headphones, hard drives, electric-car motors and wind turbines. Its name means ‘new twin’: it hid inside ‘didymium’ for decades until Carl Auer von Welsbach separated it from praseodymium in 1885. The free atom has four unpaired 4f electrons ([Xe] 4f⁴ 6s², ground term ⁵I₄). Because the 4f shell lies deep inside the atom, shielded by outer electrons, it keeps much of its orbital magnetism even in a crystal, and that helps lock the magnetisation of the magnets in place.
