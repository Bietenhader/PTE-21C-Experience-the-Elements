Promethium is the only lanthanide with no stable isotopes: the longest-lived, promethium-145, has a half-life of just 17.7 years, so any present when the Earth formed vanished long ago, and only fleeting traces arise from the fission of uranium in rocks. It was identified in the 1940s among fission products from a nuclear reactor and named after Prometheus, who stole fire from the gods. Promethium-147, a gentle beta emitter, has lit luminous dials and powered tiny nuclear batteries. The atom follows the filling rule, [Xe] 4f⁵ 6s², with five unpaired 4f electrons and the ground term ⁶H°₅/₂.
