Samarium is a silvery lanthanide named, through the mineral samarskite, after the Russian mining engineer Vasili Samarsky-Bykhovets, which makes it the first element named, if indirectly, after a real person. Samarium–cobalt magnets keep their strength at high temperatures, and geologists date ancient rocks by the slow alpha decay of samarium-147 (half-life 1.07 × 10¹¹ years) into neodymium-143. The atom holds six unpaired 4f electrons ([Xe] 4f⁶ 6s²), yet its ground level ⁷F₀ has total angular momentum J = 0: spin and orbital motion exactly cancel, so a samarium atom in its lowest level shows no hyperfine splitting.
