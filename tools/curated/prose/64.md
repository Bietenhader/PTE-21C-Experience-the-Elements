Gadolinium is a silvery lanthanide named after the Finnish chemist Johan Gadolin, through the mineral gadolinite. It captures slow neutrons better than any other element, which suits it to reactor control, and its compounds are injected as contrast agents to brighten MRI scans. It is ferromagnetic only below about room temperature, so a piece can lose its magnetism on a warm day. The atom is [Xe] 4f⁷ 5d¹ 6s², not the predicted 4f⁸ 6s²: the half-filled 4f shell holds and the extra electron goes into 5d, leaving eight unpaired electrons (⁹D°₂), a total matched only by curium.
