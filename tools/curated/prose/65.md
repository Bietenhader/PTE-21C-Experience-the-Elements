Terbium is a soft, silver-grey lanthanide and one of four elements named after the Swedish village of Ytterby, whose quarry yielded a string of rare earths. Its ions glow a vivid green, so terbium compounds supply the green in many fluorescent lamps. Terfenol-D, an alloy of terbium, dysprosium and iron, changes shape in a magnetic field more than almost any other material and drives sonar transmitters and precision actuators. The atom has nine 4f electrons ([Xe] 4f⁹ 6s²), five of them unpaired, and a ground level ⁶H°₁₅/₂ with the large total angular momentum J = 15/2.
