Dysprosium is a soft, silvery lanthanide whose name comes from the Greek for ‘hard to get at’, after the long series of painstaking separations Paul-Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran needed to detect it in 1886. Today it is quietly vital: a little dysprosium added to neodymium magnets keeps them strong at the high temperatures inside electric-car motors and wind-turbine generators. Its atom ([Xe] 4f¹⁰ 6s²) has four unpaired 4f electrons and a ground level ⁵I₈ with total angular momentum J = 8, the largest of any element, shared only with californium, which makes dysprosium atoms among the most magnetic known.
