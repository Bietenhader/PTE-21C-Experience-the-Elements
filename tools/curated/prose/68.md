Erbium is a soft, silvery lanthanide, another of the four elements named after Ytterby in Sweden; its oxide is pink and tints glass and porcelain glazes. Its great modern job is in the internet: glass fibres doped with erbium ions amplify the infrared light signals in long-distance and undersea cables directly, without turning them into electricity, because erbium ions emit just at the wavelengths where glass fibre is most transparent. The neutral atom, [Xe] 4f¹² 6s², is two electrons short of a full 4f shell, giving two unpaired electrons and the ground level ³H₆; natural erbium consists of six stable isotopes.
