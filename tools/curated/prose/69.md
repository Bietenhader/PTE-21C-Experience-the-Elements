Thulium is a soft, silver-grey lanthanide named after Thule, a classical name for the far north, and it is the scarcest of the lanthanides that have stable isotopes. That rarity long kept it a curiosity, but thulium made radioactive in a reactor serves as a compact source for portable X-ray units, and thulium-doped fibre lasers are used in surgery. All natural thulium is thulium-169, with nuclear spin 1/2. Its electron shell, [Xe] 4f¹³ 6s², lacks just one electron to fill the 4f subshell, so it behaves like a single ‘hole’ with orbital angular momentum L = 3, giving the ground level ²F°₇/₂.
