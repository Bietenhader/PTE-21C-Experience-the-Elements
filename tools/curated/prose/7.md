Nitrogen is a colourless, unreactive gas that makes up most of the air we breathe, yet it is also a building block of every protein and every strand of DNA. Plants cannot use the gas directly, because the triple bond holding the two atoms of N₂ together is extraordinarily strong; legumes such as peas and clover solve this by housing bacteria in root nodules that turn it into ammonia. A nitrogen atom, [He] 2s² 2p³, has three 2p electrons, one in each p orbital with parallel spins (ground term ⁴S°₃/₂). This half-filled subshell is so stable that nitrogen's first ionisation energy, 1402 kJ/mol, exceeds oxygen's 1314 kJ/mol.
