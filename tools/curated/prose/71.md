Lutetium is the last and densest of the lanthanides, a hard, silvery metal named after Lutetia, the Roman name for Paris. Two of its isotopes have unusual careers: natural lutetium-176, with a half-life of 3.76 × 10¹⁰ years, lets geologists date ancient rocks by its slow decay into hafnium, while reactor-made lutetium-177 is attached to molecules that seek out tumours in targeted cancer therapy. With a full 4f shell plus one 5d electron, [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹ 6s² (ground term ²D₃/₂), lutetium resembles yttrium, which is why many chemists place it, rather than lanthanum, in group 3.
