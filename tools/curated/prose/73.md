Tantalum is a hard, grey, heavy transition metal that shrugs off almost every acid, so it is used in chemical plant, surgical implants and, above all, the tiny capacitors inside phones and laptops. It is named after Tantalus of Greek myth, condemned to stand in water he could never drink, because the metal sits in acid without taking any up. Its nuclei hold a rarity: a minute fraction of natural tantalum is tantalum-180 in a long-lived excited nuclear state, the only such isomer to survive in nature since before the Earth formed, and its decay has never been observed. The atom’s ground state is [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d³ 6s², ⁴F₃/₂.
