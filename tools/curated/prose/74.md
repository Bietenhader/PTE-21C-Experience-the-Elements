Tungsten is a dense, steel-grey transition metal with the highest melting point of any metal, 3695 K, which is why it made the glowing filaments of incandescent bulbs and still forms welding electrodes and X-ray targets. Its name is Swedish for ‘heavy stone’, while its symbol, W, comes from wolfram, after the ore wolframite; tungsten carbide, extremely hard, tips drills and cutting tools. In the atom, relativity shows: chromium and molybdenum above it take a d⁵ s¹ arrangement, but tungsten keeps two 6s electrons, [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁴ 6s² (ground term ⁵D₀), because relativistic effects draw the 6s orbital inward and lower its energy.
