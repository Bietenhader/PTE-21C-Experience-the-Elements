Osmium is a bluish-white, extremely hard and brittle platinum-group metal, and the densest element, at 22.57 g/cm³, just ahead of iridium. Its name comes from the Greek for smell, because finely divided osmium slowly gives off osmium tetroxide, a volatile, pungent and highly toxic compound. That same tetroxide is a classic stain for electron microscopy: it reacts with the fatty membranes of cells, fixing them and making them stand out dark in the image. Osmium’s ground state follows the filling rule, [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁶ 6s², with four unpaired 5d electrons (ground term ⁵D₄).
