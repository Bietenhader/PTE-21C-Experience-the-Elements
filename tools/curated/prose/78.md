Platinum is a dense, silvery-white precious metal that never tarnishes in air; it is the workhorse catalyst of car exhaust converters, fuel cells and chemical plants. One of its compounds, cisplatin, was found to fight cancer by accident, when platinum electrodes in a bacterial culture produced a substance that stopped the cells from dividing; it remains a mainstay of chemotherapy. Its electrons break the filling rule: the ground state is [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁹ 6s¹ rather than 5d⁸ 6s², with the ground term ³D₃. Platinum-195, a third of natural platinum, has nuclear spin 1/2 and is widely used in NMR.
