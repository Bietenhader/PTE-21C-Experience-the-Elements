Gold is a soft, dense, yellow metal that never tarnishes, treasured for jewellery and coinage for thousands of years and now also used for corrosion-free electrical contacts. It is so malleable that it can be beaten into leaf thin enough to let greenish light through. Its colour is a relativistic effect: electrons passing close to the 79-proton nucleus move at a large fraction of the speed of light, which contracts the 6s orbital and raises the 5d levels, so gold absorbs blue light and reflects yellow; without relativity it would look silvery. Its ground configuration is [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s¹, not the predicted 5d⁹ 6s² (ground term ²S₁/₂).
