Oxygen is a reactive nonmetal and, by mass, the most common element in Earth's crust, bound up in water and in silicate rocks. Almost all the free oxygen in the air was released by photosynthesis, as algae and plants split water molecules using the energy of sunlight. Cooled below its boiling point of 90.2 K, oxygen becomes a pale-blue liquid that clings to the poles of a strong magnet. That magnetism has a quantum origin: the O₂ molecule keeps two unpaired electrons, a triplet state that simple bonding diagrams miss, and the free atom, [He] 2s² 2p⁴, also has two unpaired electrons and the ground term ³P₂.
