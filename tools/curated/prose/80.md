Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature, melting at 234.32 K (−38.83 °C). Known since antiquity and refined from the red ore cinnabar, it once filled thermometers and barometers, but its vapour and compounds are toxic. It is so dense, 13.53 g/cm³, that a lump of lead floats on it. The liquidity is a relativistic effect: its two 6s electrons ([Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s², ground term ¹S₀) are drawn close to the heavy nucleus and held so tightly, giving the highest first ionisation energy of any metal (1007 kJ/mol), that mercury atoms bond only weakly to one another.
