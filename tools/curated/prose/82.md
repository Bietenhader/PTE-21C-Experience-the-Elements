Lead is the soft, dense, bluish-white metal that people have smelted since antiquity for pipes, roofing, solder, batteries and radiation shielding, and that we now handle with care because it is a cumulative poison. Three of its four natural isotopes are endpoints: lead-206, lead-207 and lead-208 are where the decay chains of uranium-238, uranium-235 and thorium-232 finally stop, so uranium-bearing minerals slowly accumulate lead, a clock that geologists use to date rocks. Lead-208 is doubly magic: its 82 protons and 126 neutrons each complete a nuclear shell, which makes it exceptionally tightly bound.
