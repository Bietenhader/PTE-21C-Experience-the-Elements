Bismuth is a brittle, silvery metal with a faint pink tinge, used in low-melting alloys for fire sprinklers and fuses and, as its salts, in remedies for upset stomachs. Like water, it expands as it freezes, by about 3 per cent. It was long counted as the heaviest stable element, but its only natural isotope, bismuth-209, does decay, emitting an alpha particle with a half-life of about 2 × 10¹⁹ years, vastly longer than the age of the universe. The nucleus has 126 neutrons, a magic number, and spin 9/2, while the atom's three 6p electrons align their spins to give a ⁴S°₃/₂ ground term.
