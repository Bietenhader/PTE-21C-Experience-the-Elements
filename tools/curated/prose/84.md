Polonium is a rare and intensely radioactive element of group 16, which Marie and Pierre Curie traced in uranium ore in 1898 and named after Marie's native Poland; a tonne of ore contains only about 100 micrograms of it. Polonium-210, with a half-life of about 138 days, releases energy so fiercely that half a gram sealed in a capsule heats itself above 500 °C, which is why it was considered as a lightweight heat source for spacecraft. Its nucleus has the magic number of 126 neutrons. In the atom, four electrons share the 6p subshell, two of them unpaired, producing a ³P₂ ground term.
