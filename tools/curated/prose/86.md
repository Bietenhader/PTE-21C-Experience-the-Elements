Radon is a colourless, odourless, radioactive noble gas that seeps from rocks and soils containing uranium and thorium; it can collect in poorly ventilated cellars and homes, where breathing it raises the risk of lung cancer. Its longest-lived isotope, radon-222, has a half-life of about 3.8 days. Freeze it and it shines: solid radon glows a brilliant yellow, turning orange-red as it is cooled further towards the temperature of liquid air. Its filled 6p subshell gives the closed-shell ¹S₀ ground term shared by all noble gases, yet its first ionisation energy, about 1037 kJ/mol, is the lowest of any noble gas measured, low enough that radon reacts with fluorine.
