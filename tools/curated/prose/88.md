Radium is a radioactive alkaline-earth metal that Marie and Pierre Curie extracted from pitchblende in 1898; a tonne of uranium ore yields only about 0.14 grams of it. Its salts glow faintly in the dark, which once led to its use in luminous paint for watch dials. Radium-226, with a half-life of 1600 years, defined the old unit of activity, the curie, and the Curies' own laboratory notebooks are still too contaminated to handle safely. Its nucleus has a rare trick: radium-223, radium-226 and some other radium isotopes occasionally fling out a whole carbon-14 nucleus instead of an alpha particle, a process called cluster decay.
