Fluorine is a pale-yellow, poisonous gas and the most reactive of all elements; we meet it tamed, as fluoride in toothpaste, in non-stick pan coatings and in the mineral fluorite. Fluorite often glows under ultraviolet light, and it lent its name both to the element and to the word 'fluorescence'. A fluorine atom, [He] 2s² 2p⁵, is one electron short of a closed shell (ground term ²P°₃/₂), and it pulls on shared electrons harder than any other atom, with the highest Pauling electronegativity, 3.98. Its only stable isotope, fluorine-19, has nuclear spin 1/2, which makes fluorine-containing drugs easy to follow by NMR.
