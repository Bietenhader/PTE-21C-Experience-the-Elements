Thorium is a weakly radioactive, silvery actinide metal named after Thor, the Norse god of thunder, by Jöns Jacob Berzelius, who identified it in 1828 in a mineral from Norway. It is more than three times as abundant in Earth's crust as uranium. Its oxide has the highest melting point of any oxide, about 3300 °C, and gas-lamp mantles made with it glowed a dazzling white; modern mantles mostly use non-radioactive substitutes. Its main isotope, thorium-232, has a half-life of 14 billion years. In the atom, the two electrons outside the 7s² pair sit in 6d, giving [Rn] 6d² 7s² rather than the Aufbau prediction 5f² 7s², and a ³F₂ ground term.
