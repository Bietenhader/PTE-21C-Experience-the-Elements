Protactinium is a rare, toxic and strongly radioactive actinide between thorium and uranium; its name means 'before actinium', because its main isotope, protactinium-231, with a half-life of about 33,000 years, decays into actinium. The first isotope found, in 1913, vanished so quickly that its discoverers called the element brevium. Collecting a real sample was heroic work: in 1961 British chemists processed about 55 tonnes of ore to obtain some 125 grams of the pure element. In the atom one electron sits in 6d, [Rn] 5f² 6d¹ 7s², instead of the Aufbau prediction 5f³ 7s², and the large combined orbital momentum, L = 7, gives the ground term the rare letter K: ⁴K₁₁/₂.
