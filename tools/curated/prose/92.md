Uranium is the dense, silvery-grey actinide metal that fuels nuclear reactors, named in 1789 after the then newly discovered planet Uranus; in 1896 Henri Becquerel discovered radioactivity while studying its salts. Nature built reactors first: about 1.7 billion years ago, uranium ore at Oklo in Gabon sustained fission chain reactions on its own, because uranium-235 then made up about 3 per cent of natural uranium instead of today's 0.72 per cent. In the atom, one electron goes into 6d rather than 5f, giving [Rn] 5f³ 6d¹ 7s² against the Aufbau prediction 5f⁴ 7s², and the ground term ⁵L°₆ carries a total orbital angular momentum of L = 8.
