Plutonium is a radioactive actinide metal first made at Berkeley in 1940–41, whose discovery stayed secret until after the Second World War; plutonium-239, with a half-life of about 24,000 years, drives nuclear weapons and some reactors, while plutonium-238 powers spacecraft far from the Sun. As a metal it is notoriously awkward: at ordinary pressure it passes through six crystal forms between room temperature and its melting point, with densities from about 16 to nearly 20 grams per cubic centimetre. Its ground configuration, [Rn] 5f⁶ 7s², holds six unpaired 5f electrons with parallel spins, yet spin and orbital momentum cancel exactly in the ⁷F₀ ground level, with J = 0.
