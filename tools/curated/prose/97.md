Berkelium is a synthetic actinide metal, first made in December 1949 at Berkeley, California, by firing alpha particles at americium-241, and named after the city. It is so scarce that the first compound anyone could actually see, prepared in 1962, weighed about three billionths of a gram. Its chief use today is as raw material for heavier elements: berkelium-249, which has a half-life of 330 days, was the target from which tennessine was made. The atom's nine 5f electrons, in [Rn] 5f⁹ 7s², leave five spins unpaired and give a ⁶H°₁₅/₂ ground term, whose total angular momentum, J = 15/2, is among the largest of any element.
