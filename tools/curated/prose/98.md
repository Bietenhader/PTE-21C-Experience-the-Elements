Californium is a synthetic actinide metal, made at Berkeley in 1950 by striking curium-242 with helium ions, and named after the state. Its isotope californium-252, with a half-life of about 2.6 years, is an astonishingly strong neutron emitter: a single microgram releases around 170 million neutrons every minute, which makes it a compact source for locating water and oil layers in boreholes and for detecting gold and silver in ores. The atom's ten 5f electrons, [Rn] 5f¹⁰ 7s², form a ⁵I₈ ground level; its J = 8 equals that of dysprosium, the lanthanide above it, and no element has a larger total angular momentum in its ground state.
