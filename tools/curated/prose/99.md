Einsteinium is a synthetic actinide named after Albert Einstein, and it was found in an extraordinary place: debris from the first large hydrogen-bomb test, in the Pacific in November 1952, where uranium-238 nuclei had captured as many as 15 neutrons in an instant before climbing the table through a series of beta decays. The discovery was kept secret for several years. Einsteinium is still made only in tiny amounts, and its intense radiation damages any sample from within; einsteinium-253 has a half-life of about 20 days and the longest-lived isotope, einsteinium-252, about 470 days.
